### Round 2: Transparent AI System

**Scenario:** College Scholarship AI System

**AI Decision:** Sarah Miller – Scholarship Denied.

**AI Evaluation:**

| Criteria | Weight | Sarah's Score |
|---|---:|---:|
| GPA | 40% | 76/100 |
| Standardized Test Scores | 30% | 45/100 |
| Volunteer Work | 20% | 85/100 |
| Leadership Experience | 10% | 20/100 |

**Overall Score:** 61/100  
**Scholarship Threshold:** 75/100

**User Questions and Responses**

**1. Why was I denied?**

I was denied because my overall score was 61/100, which is below the required 75/100. My standardized test and leadership scores lowered my total.

**2. What can I do to improve my chances?**

I can improve my chances by providing standardized test scores and gaining leadership experience. I should also continue my volunteer work and maintain my GPA.

**3. Was my application reviewed fairly?**

The AI used the same criteria to evaluate applications, but I would need to know whether those criteria treat all applicants fairly.

**4. Can I appeal this decision?**

I would like to contact the scholarship committee to ask whether I can appeal the decision or request a human review.

**5. What criteria are used for selection?**

The AI evaluates GPA (40%), standardized test scores (30%), volunteer work (20%), and leadership experience (10%).

### Round 3: Medical AI Diagnosis Assistant

**AI Decision:** Patient shows a 78% probability of having Type 2 Diabetes.

**Factors Considered:**
- Blood glucose levels: Elevated (185 mg/dL).
- BMI: 32.5.
- Age: 45.
- Family history: Diabetes in both parents.
- Physical activity: Sedentary.
- Symptoms: Excessive thirst and frequent urination.

**Recommendation:** Consult a doctor for confirmatory tests and treatment options.

**User Questions and Responses**

**1. How certain is this diagnosis?**

The AI estimates a 78% probability, but this is not a confirmed diagnosis. A doctor must perform further tests to confirm it.

**2. What specific factors led to this conclusion?**

The AI considered my elevated blood glucose, BMI, age, family history, low physical activity, and reported symptoms.

**3. What should I do next?**

I should consult a doctor, undergo confirmatory tests, and discuss the results with a healthcare professional.

**4. Could this be wrong?**

Yes. AI predictions are not always accurate. Other medical conditions or factors could affect the results, so a doctor must confirm the diagnosis.

**5. What would happen if I change my lifestyle?**

Improving physical activity, eating balanced meals, and following medical advice may help reduce diabetes risk and improve overall health.

### Group Discussion Questions

**1. Should all AI systems be required to explain their decisions?**

Yes, AI systems should explain important decisions so people can understand the reasoning, identify possible mistakes, and challenge unfair outcomes.

**2. Are there situations where transparency might be harmful?**

Yes. Revealing too much information could expose private data or create security risks. Therefore, transparency should be balanced with privacy and safety.

**3. How detailed should explanations be for different types of decisions?**

Explanations should depend on the importance of the decision. High-impact decisions, such as healthcare, education, and hiring, need detailed explanations, while simple decisions may need shorter explanations.
